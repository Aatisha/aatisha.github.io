← Work / MyWorker.AI

An AI sales agent that lets founders try the product, instead of touring it.

MyWorker.AI automates the top of the sales funnel for startups, but its own website was losing curious visitors to long forms and silence. As product designer and research lead, I designed Alex, an AI video agent who greets each visitor, finds them real leads and drafts their outreach in about five minutes, and earns the sign-up along the way.

01 · Context

Startups can’t afford a sales team. MyWorker.AI wants to be one.

MyWorker.AI researches prospects, writes personalised outreach and books meetings for small teams, so founders can spend their time closing instead of chasing. The business needed its own website to prove that promise to every visitor.

  1. Convert more visitorsTurn first-time visitors into qualified leads.
  2. Give founders time backTake repetitive sales work off their plate.
  3. Scale without hiringGrow the pipeline without growing headcount.

02 · Problem

Curious visitors were leaving before they ever became leads.

To see any value, a visitor had to fill in their company, their customer persona and their sales details across several steps. Then they waited, with no idea when or whether leads would arrive. Curiosity turned into fatigue, and nothing about it felt personal.

  1. Effort before value

    Long forms stood between a visitor and the first useful result.

  2. Silence after

    No sign of when leads would come, or what happened next.

  3. No human touch

    Nothing like the guided, personal pitch a founder would give.

03 · Research

AI video agents were new ground, so I led research before we drew a screen.

I planned the research around three questions: which jobs an AI sales agent is most valuable for, how comfortable people are with a lifelike AI, and what a realistic first version looks like.

  1. Market14 competitors, from 11x.ai, Artisan and Warmly to Drift and Ada, plus a PESTLE review of AI regulation and adoption
  2. Literature8 papers on human–AI collaboration, anthropomorphism, transparency and the uncanny valley
  3. Founders7 interviews across e-commerce, real estate, creative and service businesses; 160+ notes synthesised

Competitors automated the task, and ignored the feeling

AI sales tools competed on how much they automate. Few considered how it feels to hand a stranger your company’s first impression, and most dropped small teams into heavy setup. No one had combined a lifelike video agent with a language model to sell with empathy. That was our gap.

Founders wanted help, not a stand-in

Every interview circled the same tension between automation and authenticity. Founders were eager for AI to research and draft for them, and wary of anything that replaced them in front of a customer.

“I’m not really a fan of entirely removing humans from customer support.”Founder, interview

04 · Insights

Founders didn’t want AI that pretends to be them. They wanted AI that works for them.

Clustering the notes from interviews and papers left four insights. Each one became a test for the design: if a screen broke one of them, it went back.

  1. Time is the scarcest resource

    Founders judge a tool in minutes. Anything that looks like setup, or a tour, gets skipped.

  2. Automate the mechanics, keep the voice

    They want AI to do the research and drafting, while they keep the personal touch and the final say.

  3. Human-aware, not human-like

    Warmth helps; pretending hurts. People trust an agent that is clearly AI, and 58% want full transparency about AI use.

  4. Trust is earned in the open

    People trust AI more when it shows its role, its reasoning and its limits, and lets them check its sources.

How might weearn a founder’s trust fast enough to turn a curious visit into a qualified lead?

05 · Testing

We tested early with founders. The first round broke our plan.

From Crazy 8s and storyboards we chose four use cases: a digital concierge, guided product discovery, lead qualification and booking, and AI follow-ups. Each designer mapped one end to end before we merged them into a single flow. We took it to mid-fidelity quickly, because the feel of a human-like presence mattered as much as the flow.

06 · Decisions

Six decisions that shaped Alex.

Each one traces back to an insight or a test. The second was the biggest call of the project.

Decision 1 From insight 3

Give the agent a face, and say plainly that it’s AI.

Tension
A lifelike avatar adds the warmth chatbots lack, but it can tip into uncanny, or feel like a trick.
Chose
A calm video agent, Alex, always labelled Powered by AI, with a reminder to check details and quick feedback on every reply.
Why
The research was clear: people trust an AI more when it’s honest about being one. Human-aware, not human-like.

Decision 2 From round 1

Kill the demo. Let them do the work instead.

First design
Alex greets the visitor, plays a product tour, then offers to book a meeting.
Chose
Alex says let’s do it together: reads the visitor’s website, finds five real leads and drafts their emails, live, inside the conversation.
Why
Founders didn’t want to be shown the value; they wanted to feel it. Their own leads are a better pitch than any tour.
Before
  1. Greet
  2. Watch a demo
  3. Book a meeting
After
  1. Greet
  2. Generate leads
  3. Draft emails
  4. Book a meeting

To fix the chatbot feeling too, the whole flow happens inside Alex’s frame: no side panels, no separate chat window.

Decision 3 From round 2

Show who you’re looking for before showing who you found.

Testing showed
Founders couldn’t judge the leads without knowing how they were matched: by company size, role or industry.
Chose
Alex first shows the Ideal Customer Profile it read from your site, with a one-tap Fix ICP details, before searching.
Why
A lead is only qualified if the founder agrees with the criteria. It turns an AI guess into a shared decision.
Before After

Decision 4 From round 2

Earn the email. Don’t ask for it up front.

Tension
The business needs an email to convert a visitor. Founders hesitated to hand one to an AI in the middle of a chat.
Chose
Give the value first, the leads and drafts, then ask, saying exactly what the email is for: creating an account to send them.
Why
Sharing an email felt right only when it meant signing up. Asking at that moment makes the lead capture the conversion.

Decision 5 From insight 4

Show the work, and admit the limits.

Tension
Finding leads takes time and AI can be wrong. A spinner and a confident answer both cost trust.
Chose
A thinking state that lists what Alex is checking, sources behind every finding, and a plain “I don’t have that” with an alternative.
Why
Predictability is trust. Users relax when they can see what the AI is doing, why, and where it stops.

Decision 6 From insight 2

Measure trust, not just leads.

Tension
Founders handing their first impression to an AI need to know how it’s doing, not just how many leads it found.
Chose
Three dashboards: engagement and conversion, the agent’s own performance, and business outcomes.
Why
Edit rates, feedback and drop-off are trust signals. They show the team where Alex needs work, and founders when to step in.

07 · Refinements

Small fixes from round two, each from something founders did.

  • SawVisitors glanced past the avatar when it first appeared.

    ChangedAn opening message that says what Alex is for: unlock 5 leads for your company in 5 minutes.

  • SawFounders skimmed long replies and lost the thread.

    ChangedReplies cut to one idea and one question, with the next step as a button.

  • SawFounders wanted their voice in the emails before trusting them.

    ChangedEvery draft opens to edit, and can be reworked with Alex in the chat.

08 · Craft

Trust lives in the in-between moments.

  • Google Veo promptMedium shot, eye level. She raises a slight wave, smiles and says, “Hi, I’m Alex…” Her tone is soft and inviting; her body language open and welcoming.
    Directing the avatar

    I wrote and tuned the video prompts for every state, from listening to thinking, so Alex feels present without being uncanny.

  • Nothing hidden behind hover

    Key controls were getting lost behind hover states, especially on phones. Every core action is now visible by default.

  • Transcripts to keep

    Every conversation is saved, with its leads and drafts, so a chat becomes something a team can revisit and share.

09 · Outcome

A first conversation that delivers value before it asks for anything.

In round two, founders finished the flow and described Alex as “approachable” and “efficient”. The design gave MyWorker.AI a way to show its product working on a visitor’s own business, and a way to watch, in numbers, whether people trust it.

  • ~6 hrsa week saved per founder, estimated
  • 0forms before the first useful result
  • 3dashboards to watch trust, not just volume

10 · Reflection

What I’d take to the next project.

  1. Research is a confrontation, not a validation. The demo was our favourite idea. Two founders skipping it taught us more than any amount of agreement would have.
  2. I’d test the riskiest idea at lower fidelity. We polished the demo flow before learning nobody wanted it. A rough clickable test in week two would have found that sooner.
  3. Next, I’d measure the real funnel. Visit to lead to account, and how often founders send Alex’s emails unedited. Then the next steps: a handoff to a human, and letting Alex act, not just suggest, with the founder’s approval.
