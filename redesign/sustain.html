← Work / Sustain Dane

Turning “I should do something” about climate change into a first step, with a friend.

Most people care about climate change; far fewer act on it. For Sustain Dane, a sustainability nonprofit in Madison, I designed a mobile app around that gap: pick one small goal, take it on with a partner who keeps you honest, and when you finish, plant a real tree. I led it end to end, from desk research to a tested prototype.

01 · Context

It started with a February that felt like spring.

In early 2024, Madison had a February that didn’t feel like winter. Feeling warm on my walk to class was the moment the climate news stopped feeling distant, and the moment I wondered what one person could actually do. Sustain Dane works on that question for Dane County, helping people live more sustainably. The brief: design an app that helps people make sustainable choices and keep making them.

02 · Problem

People care about the climate. Caring isn’t turning into action.

Climate headlines are everywhere, and they mostly leave people worried and unsure what to do. Yale’s long-running survey makes the gap concrete: across every kind of action, the share of people who say they would act is two to five times the share who did.

The question for the design wasn’t how to make people care more. It was how to get people who already care to take the first step, and the second.

03 · Research

I looked for what stands between intention and action.

  1. Desk researchStudies on climate attitudes and behaviour, from Yale, Harvard Business Review, PwC and the World Economic Forum
  2. Competitive reviewEarth Hero, Ailuna and JouleBug, three apps trying to change everyday habits
  3. SynthesisAffinity mapping across plantation, lifestyle, awareness, achievements and metrics; a persona and a storyboard

Five barriers, and two reasons for hope

The barriers to action kept coming back to the same five: a lack of resources, opportunities, awareness, fun and accountability, and a sense of achievement. But the research also pointed somewhere useful. Gen Z and Millennial shoppers are 27% more likely to buy from sustainable brands, and digital tools could cut global emissions by up to 20% by 2050. The people most open to change are already on their phones.

Each competitor solved one barrier, and created another

  • Earth HeroClear actions, done aloneSpecific actions and goal tracking, but little community and nothing that makes it fun.
  • AilunaGood habits, no payoffBehaviour science and a simple interface, but rigid goals and no rewards.
  • JouleBugFun, but noisyPoints, badges and sharing, but complex to navigate and thin on learning.

No one combined the three things the research asked for: learning what to do, someone to do it with, and a reward that feels real.

Who it’s for

I brought it together in a persona, Julia, a 30-year-old marketing specialist. She wants small, real actions that fit her life, people to do them with, and to feel her effort counts. She’s let down by resources she can’t trust and by doing it alone.

“I want to make a difference, but sometimes it feels overwhelming to know where to start.”Julia, persona

04 · Insights

People don’t need more information. They need a first step and someone beside them.

  1. The barrier is the first step

    People already care. What stops them is not knowing where to start, and feeling that one small action won’t matter.

  2. People act when someone asks

    Yale’s “would act” numbers are for when someone you like and respect asks you. Accountability is social.

  3. Effort has to feel like it counted

    Without a visible result, small actions feel pointless, and people stop.

  4. Neighbours teach better than headlines

    Global news raises worry. A local story of someone like you shows what’s actually doable.

How might wehelp people who already care about the climate take their first sustainable step, and keep going, in a way that feels fun and accountable?

05 · Testing

Paper first, because it’s cheap to be wrong on paper.

I mapped three task flows: setting a goal with a partner, learning from resources, and being rewarded for a finished goal. Then I drew every screen on paper and put the prototype in front of people before opening Figma.

Paper prototype test

Tested
All three flows, on paper
With
3 participants
Asked
Can people set a goal, find a partner, and learn something along the way?
Found
Four problems. The home screen wasn’t about you; partners were hard to find; partner profiles gave no reason to trust them; and stories could be read but not joined.

06 · Decisions

Six decisions that shaped the app.

Each traces back to an insight or to the paper test. Together they follow one journey: learn, commit with someone, and celebrate.

Decision 1 From insight 1

Start with one small goal, not a carbon footprint.

Options
A carbon footprint calculator, a feed of daily challenges, or a short list of concrete goals.
Chose
Curated goals like recycle old clothes, plant a tree or turn off the AC, each with why it matters, set once or on chosen days.
Why
A footprint tells you how bad things are. A goal tells you what to do tomorrow. I deliberately left footprint tracking for later, once a habit exists.

Decision 2 From insight 2

Ask “solo or team up?” before anything else.

Tension
Accountability works best with a person, but requiring a partner would block anyone who wants to start alone.
Chose
The first step of every goal is a choice: Individual or Team Up. Team goals show both people’s progress side by side.
Why
The research said people act when someone they respect asks them. Making it the first question makes teaming up the natural path, without making it mandatory.

Decision 3 From testing

Make a partner easy to find, and worth trusting.

Testing showed
People got lost in one long list of partners, and couldn’t tell whether a partner would actually follow through.
Chose
Recent, Favourites and All filters to find people fast, and profiles with eco badges, goals created and achieved, photos, stories and a Message button.
Why
You only commit to someone you believe in. A track record turns a stranger into a partner you trust.

Decision 4 From testing

Make the home screen about your goals, not our events.

Testing showed
The first home screen led with events. People didn’t see themselves in it, or how to start a goal.
Chose
A greeting, this week at a glance, today’s goals with a clear Add Goal, and Sustain Dane’s events moved below.
Why
A habit app lives or dies on the daily return. What you promised to do today should be the first thing you see.

Decision 5 From insight 3

Celebrate with a real tree, not just a badge.

Tension
Points and badges feel hollow for something as real as the climate. But asking for money after someone succeeds could spoil the moment.
Chose
A finished goal earns a certificate from Sustain Dane to download and share, then an optional offer to plant a tree in your name, with Cancel as easy as Confirm.
Why
The reward is more climate action, so it feels like it counted. The offer comes at the moment of pride, and it gives the nonprofit a way to fund planting.

Decision 6 From insight 4 and testing

Teach with neighbours’ stories, not headlines.

Tension
People need to learn what helps, but more climate news mostly adds worry. And in testing, stories could only be read.
Chose
An Explore hub of local sustainability stories and guides like Recycle Better, sortable, with Share your story to add your own.
Why
A Madison neighbour biking to work is more persuasive than a melting glacier. And sharing your own story turns readers into examples for others.

07 · Craft

Soft, green and one action at a time.

  • Flows before screens

    Three task flows, one per journey, mapped screen by screen before anything was drawn.

  • A component library

    Cards, buttons and inputs built as auto-layout components and variants, so every screen speaks the same language.

  • A calm visual tone

    Soft greens and illustrations instead of alarm red. The app should feel hopeful, not like another piece of bad news.

08 · Outcome

A complete concept: learn, commit with someone, celebrate.

The final prototype covers four journeys end to end: onboarding, exploring stories and resources, setting a goal with a partner, and finishing it with a certificate and a tree. Every problem from testing was fixed before high fidelity.

09 · Reflection

What I’d take to the next project.

  1. The most important screen had two buttons. “Individual” or “Team Up” carries the whole idea of the app. Information architecture decided more than visual design did.
  2. I’d test the money moment. Offering a paid tree right after a win could feel like a gift or like a sales pitch. I’d test the wording and timing with real users before shipping it.
  3. Next, I’d measure the habit, not the session. A habit app is judged over weeks. I’d run a two-week diary study, then add footprint tracking, impact dashboards and eco badges once people keep coming back.
